少年 · For young people

Shōnen

The most popular anime genre. Young protagonists who grow by pushing their limits, making friends, and facing increasingly powerful adversaries.

View details →

⚔️ Shōnen

Target audience

Boys aged 12 to 18, though its popularity spans all demographics.

Subgenres

Action Martial arts Sports Adventure

Iconic works

  • Dragon Ball (1986) — Akira Toriyama
  • Naruto (2002) — Masashi Kishimoto
  • My Hero Academia (2016) — Kōhei Horikoshi
  • Demon Slayer (2019) — Koyoharu Gotouge

少女 · For young girls

Shōjo

Narratives centered on relationships, emotions, and personal growth. Visually and aesthetically elaborate, with expressive eyes and pastel palettes.

View details →

🌸 Shōjo

Target audience

Girls aged 12 to 18; also very popular among adults.

Subgenres

Romance Mahō Shōjo Drama Slice of Life

Iconic works

  • Sailor Moon (1992) — Naoko Takeuchi
  • Cardcaptor Sakura (1998) — CLAMP
  • Fruits Basket (2001/2019) — Natsuki Takaya
  • Nana (2006) — Ai Yazawa

青年 · For adult men

Seinen

Anime aimed at adults with darker, more complex and philosophical themes. Uncompromising violence, politics, psychology, and moral ambiguity.

View details →

🔪 Seinen

Target audience

Men aged 18 to 40. Mature content and narratives without a guaranteed happy ending.

Subgenres

Thriller Horror Cyberpunk Historical

Iconic works

  • Berserk (1997) — Kentaro Miura
  • Ghost in the Shell (1995) — Mamoru Oshii
  • Vinland Saga (2019) — Makoto Yukimura
  • Monster (2004) — Naoki Urasawa

女性 · For adult women

Josei

Romance and life stories aimed at adult women. More realistic and emotionally mature than Shōjo, with complex relationships and characters with history.

View details →

💐 Josei

Target audience

Women aged 18 to 40; emphasis on emotional realism and adult relationships.

Subgenres

Adult romance Workplace drama Slice of Life

Iconic works

  • Chihayafuru (2011) — Yuki Suetsugu
  • Nana (2006) — Ai Yazawa
  • Paradise Kiss (2005) — Ai Yazawa
  • Honey and Clover (2005) — Chica Umino

異世界 · Another world

Isekai

A character is transported, reincarnated, or trapped in a world different from their own. The most booming genre of the last decade.

View details →

🌀 Isekai

Main characteristic

Protagonist transported to another world, often with special powers or advantageous knowledge from the original world.

Variants

Reincarnation Video game Medieval fantasy Reverse Isekai

Iconic works

  • Sword Art Online (2012) — Reki Kawahara
  • Re:Zero (2016) — Tappei Nagatsuki
  • Overlord (2015) — Kugane Maruyama
  • Mushoku Tensei (2021) — Rifujin na Magonote

メカ · Giant robots

Mecha

Humans pilot giant robots to face large‑scale threats. From pure entertainment to philosophical allegories about war and humanity.

View details →

🤖 Mecha

Two main currents

Super Robot: fantastic robots with special powers (Mazinger Z). Real Robot: realistic military vehicles with political implications (Gundam).

Subgenres

Super Robot Real Robot Military Sci-Fi

Iconic works

  • Mazinger Z (1972) — Go Nagai
  • Mobile Suit Gundam (1979) — Yoshiyuki Tomino
  • Neon Genesis Evangelion (1995) — Hideaki Anno
  • Gurren Lagann (2007) — Gainax

日常 · Daily life

Slice of Life

Stories about the ordinary lives of normal characters. No great battles or magical powers — just the weight and beauty of the everyday.

View details →

☕ Slice of Life

Essence of the genre

Highlights the beauty of small moments: an afternoon with friends, the first day of school, cooking a meal. Conflict is emotional, not physical.

Frequent combinations

Romance Comedy School drama CGDCT

Iconic works

  • Clannad (2007) — Key / KyoAni
  • K-On! (2009) — Kakifly
  • March Comes in Like a Lion (2016)
  • Barakamon (2014) — Satsuki Yoshino

心理 · Psychological

Psychological

The real battlefield is the mind. Intellectual power games, fragmented identities, and endings that reconfigure everything seen before.

View details →

🧠 Psychological

Narrative resources

Unreliable narrators, non‑linear timelines, character/shadow duality, collapse of perceived reality.

Subgenres

Thriller Psychological horror Suspense

Iconic works

  • Death Note (2006) — Tsugumi Ohba
  • Paranoia Agent (2004) — Satoshi Kon
  • Serial Experiments Lain (1998)
  • Made in Abyss (2017) — Akihito Tsukushi

スポーツ · Sports

Sports

Self‑improvement through sports. Teamwork, legendary rivalries, and moments that ignite passion even in those who don't follow real sports.

View details →

🏆 Sports

Keys to the genre

Use of sport as a metaphor for growth. The rival is as important as the protagonist — no one reaches the top alone.

Sports represented

Volleyball Basketball Soccer Boxing Cycling

Iconic works

  • Haikyuu!! (2014) — Haruichi Furudate
  • Kuroko no Basket (2012) — Tadatoshi Fujimaki
  • Blue Lock (2022) — Muneyuki Kaneshiro
  • Hajime no Ippo (2000) — George Morikawa

魔法少女 · Magical girl

Mahō Shōjo

Female protagonists who gain magical powers to protect what they love. From the innocent fantasy of the 80s to the dark modern deconstruction.

View details →

✨ Mahō Shōjo

Evolution of the genre

Born as innocent fantasy (Himitsu no Akko‑chan, 1969). In 2011 Madoka Magica radically deconstructed it, showing the horror behind the magical contract.

Defining elements

Transformation Magical pets Love and friendship Deconstruction

Iconic works

  • Sailor Moon (1992) — Naoko Takeuchi
  • Cardcaptor Sakura (1998) — CLAMP
  • Puella Magi Madoka Magica (2011)
  • Pretty Cure (2004–present)

冒険 · Adventure

Adventure

Epic journeys into the unknown. The world is vast, dangers are real, and each story arc reveals a new layer of the constructed universe.

View details →

🗺️ Adventure

What defines the genre

Elaborate world‑building, extensive travels, and a group of characters that grows with the protagonist. The journey matters as much as the destination.

Frequent combinations

Fantasy Pirates Dungeon RPG

Iconic works

  • One Piece (1999) — Eiichiro Oda
  • Hunter × Hunter (1999) — Yoshihiro Togashi
  • Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood (2009)
  • Magi: The Labyrinth of Magic (2012)

超自然 · Supernatural

Supernatural

Gods, demons, spirits, and the inexplicable coexist with the everyday world. Deeply rooted in Shintoism and Japanese folklore.

View details →

👻 Supernatural

Cultural roots

Japanese folklore —yokai, oni, kami— is the inexhaustible source of this genre. The boundary between the human world and the spiritual is always porous.

Variants

Horror Yokai Exorcism Gods

Iconic works

  • Jujutsu Kaisen (2020) — Gege Akutami
  • Noragami (2014) — Adachitoka
  • Natsume's Book of Friends (2008)
  • Mononoke (2007) — Toei Animation
